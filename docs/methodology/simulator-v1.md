# Riksdag scenario simulator v1

Classification: **MODEL**. The simulator is a deterministic scenario calculator. It is not a poll, forecast, probability or prediction of the 2026 election.

Engine version: `riksdag-2026-v1.0.0`

Scenario model: `pv-riksdag-scenario@1.0.0`

Data cutoff: 2026-08-21

## Plain-language interpretation

The user enters a national vote share for each of the eight parties represented after the 2022 election. The initial values reproduce the official 2022 national percentages and the controls accept 0.01 percentage-point increments. Politicalverse projects those shares across the 29 Riksdag constituencies using each party's official 2022 geographic vote pattern. It then applies the Swedish seat-allocation rules and the official number of fixed constituency seats for the 2026 election.

The result answers one narrow question: **what seat allocation does this rules engine produce if these national shares occur and the 2022 geographic pattern is retained?** It does not estimate how likely that scenario is.

## Official inputs

- 2018 final national and constituency results, plus fixed and adjustment mandates, from Valmyndigheten.
- 2022 final constituency results and Valmyndigheten's final Riksdag decision.
- Valmyndigheten's official 2026 allocation of the 310 fixed seats across 29 constituencies.
- The current rules described by Valmyndigheten and chapter 14 of the Swedish Election Act (`Vallag 2005:837`).

Every downloaded source URL and SHA-256 checksum is pinned in `data/raw/valmyndigheten/seat-source-manifest.json`. The normalized model input is generated by `npm run data:import:seats` and verified by `npm run data:verify`.

## Electoral-rule algorithm

1. A party qualifies nationally at **at least 4%** of valid votes. A party below 4% may participate in the fixed-seat allocation in a constituency where it receives **at least 12%**; that exception does not qualify it for adjustment seats.
2. The **310 fixed constituency seats** are allocated one at a time in each constituency with the modified odd-numbers method. The first divisor is `1.2`; subsequent divisors are `3, 5, 7, ...`.
3. A national total allocation is calculated using the same method. If a party has excess fixed seats, the engine returns the party's lowest-comparison-figure seat where the law permits and reallocates it within that constituency to an underrepresented party.
4. The **39 adjustment seats** bridge the gap between fixed seats and the national total. Each party's adjustment seats go to the constituencies where its next comparison figure is highest. For a constituency where the party has no seat, the first adjustment-seat divisor is `1`.

The majority reference is 175 of 349 seats. Coalition arithmetic includes only parties selected by the user and does not encode preferred or assumed political blocs.

Exact equal comparison figures are resolved by drawing lots in an official allocation. For reproducibility, Politicalverse uses stable registry order and exposes a warning whenever this fallback occurs.

## Scenario projection

The entered percentages are mapped to a synthetic electorate of 10,000,000 valid votes so hundredths of a percentage point remain exact. For each party, those votes are distributed between constituencies in proportion to that party's final 2022 constituency votes. The residual “Other parties” share is distributed using the corresponding 2022 residual pattern and is not treated as one seat-eligible party.

Integer votes are assigned with the largest-remainder method. Ties in remainder allocation use stable constituency-code order. These steps are deterministic: identical inputs produce identical outputs.

## Historical backtests

The independent engine is run against official historical constituency votes and fixed-seat structures before hypothetical scenarios are exposed.

| Election | Fixed seats reproduced | Adjustment seats reproduced | Final party totals reproduced | Tie fallback |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2018 | 310 / 310 | 39 / 39 | 8 / 8 parties exactly | No |
| 2022 | 310 / 310 | 39 / 39 | 8 / 8 parties exactly | No |

The tests compare each party's fixed, adjustment and total seat counts—not only the 349-seat sum.

## Limitations

- The geographic projection assumes each party's relative constituency pattern remains as in 2022. Real 2026 geography can change.
- “Other parties” is a residual containing multiple parties. The model assumes none of those unmodeled parties independently qualifies for seats.
- The model does not include polls, turnout change, tactical voting, uncertainty, candidate selection or person votes.
- The 12% constituency exception is implemented, but the default 2022 geographic scaling does not imply that a below-threshold party will necessarily reach it.
- Seat allocation is not equivalent to government formation. The coalition tool performs arithmetic only.
